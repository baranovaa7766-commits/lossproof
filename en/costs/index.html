How to export the files

You need the history of executed trades, not orders: it has a fee for every trade. Use CSV. There's no need to delete UID or wallet columns: the file stays with you, and only trades, fees and dates go into the calculation.

Bybit

  • Derivatives trades — Orders → Derivatives → Trade History → Export. The same file contains funding payments and liquidations.
  • Spot trades — Orders → Spot → Trade History → Export.
  • Closed positions — Orders → Derivatives → Closed P&L → Export. Needed for your win rate and break-even threshold.
  • Transaction log — Assets → Unified Trading Account → History Records → Transaction Log → Export. Adds options fees, coin conversions inside the account and borrowing interest.

One export covers up to 6 months and 10,000 records within the last 2 years; for a longer period make several exports and upload them all — we remove the overlaps. The fallback is Account → Data Export, where a file can take 1–3 days. More in Bybit's help centre.

OKX beta

  • Trading history — Assets → Order center → Trading history → Download, CSV format.
  • Position history — Order center → Position history → Download. It has the fee and funding for every position.

We built the OKX format from OKX's official help page and haven't checked it against a real export yet, so OKX results are marked beta. A copied download link works for one hour.

How we calculate

  • Fees come straight from your files. We convert amounts to dollars: USDT and USDC count as $1, and a fee in another coin uses that coin's price from your own trades at the nearest moment. If there's no price, we don't guess — the fee is listed separately.
  • No double counting. On Bybit the same trade appears in the trade history, the transaction log and Closed P&L. We take the most detailed file and use the others only for periods it doesn't cover. Uploading the same file twice won't double the totals either.
  • Maker and taker. A market order is always a taker trade. A limit order counts as maker if its rate is clearly below the rate of market trades in the same file — that means it rested in the book.
  • Break-even uses your closed positions: given your own average win and loss after costs, what share of winning positions you need to come out even.
  • Rate comparison uses the exchanges' published base rates, with no VIP tiers or discounts. Actual rates depend on your country and account, so you can change them right in the table.
  • This isn't an assessment of your trading. We don't advise where or how to trade and don't give investment recommendations — we only count the costs in your files.
