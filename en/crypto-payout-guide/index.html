How to Withdraw a Prop Firm Payout Into Rubles: a Crypto Route Guide

Wise and Revolut don't work for residents of Russia: EU sanctions (tightened by the 19th package in late 2025) check identity and citizenship. So traders in Russia and the CIS often receive prop-firm payouts in crypto and then exchange them for rubles or a local currency. Here is how such a withdrawal works and how much is lost at each stage. This is reference information, not instructions or a recommendation of specific services.

For Russian residents: under Law No. 282-FZ, from 1 July 2027 digital currency (crypto) transactions will be allowed only through digital currency circulation organisers. The exchanges and cash-out services on this site are foreign companies. This is reference material: once it is known who receives that status, we will update the routes. Details

Two stages instead of one

Unlike a direct Wise/Revolut conversion, a crypto route is always two separate steps, and each one takes its own cut:

Stage 1 — the exchange. USDT is bought or received on a crypto exchange. The fee here is usually small (a spread of around 0.3–0.4% plus a network fee), and large exchanges are close to each other.

Stage 2 — the cash-out service. USDT is exchanged for rubles or a local currency through a cash-out service. This is usually where most of the cost hides — the spread is noticeably higher than at the exchange.

Your final amount is the starting sum minus the losses at both stages combined. You can work it out for your amount in the transfer calculator — it accounts for both stages.

Exchanges

Licences, fees and what the exchanges' user agreements say about Russia are collected in one table on the "Compare exchanges" page. All exchanges are shown the same way there: we don't pick an exchange for you.

Cash-out services

We take cash-out service tariffs from their official pages and re-check them automatically every night, but none of them publishes a tariff API — verify on the service's site before a large withdrawal. Fees, currencies and timing are on the "Compare Cash-Out Services" page.

P2P trades

We don't cover P2P trades with private sellers and the calculator doesn't include them: for Russian residents they carry the risk of card blocks under Law No. 115-FZ and liability for handing your card or account to third parties (acting as a money mule).

E-wallets as an intermediate step

We don't suggest Payeer as an intermediate step: since 25 November 2025 the EU has banned transactions with it (19th sanctions package against Russia). Transfers linked to sanctioned services may be delayed or blocked in an exchange's screening.

Bank transfer — for contrast

Bank transfer remains technically available, and it is shown among the routes above purely for contrast: a bank typically bakes a rate markup well below mid-market into the transfer, plus a flat fee. Compared to crypto routes, it's usually the most expensive option in absolute terms.
