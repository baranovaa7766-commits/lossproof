How to Withdraw a Prop Firm Payout Into Rubles: a Crypto Route Guide

Wise and Revolut don't work for residents of Russia — EU sanctions (tightened by the 19th package in late 2025) block based on identity/citizenship verification, not IP, so a VPN doesn't help. For most traders in Russia and the CIS, the real path is buying USDT on an exchange and converting it to rubles or a local currency through an off-ramp service. Here's how it works and what each stage actually costs.

Two stages instead of one

Unlike a direct Wise/Revolut conversion, a crypto route is always two separate steps, and each one takes its own cut:

Stage 1 — the exchange. You buy or receive USDT on a crypto exchange. The fee here is usually small (a spread of around 0.3-0.4% plus a small network fee) — all three exchanges below are competitive with each other.

Stage 2 — the off-ramp. You convert USDT into rubles or a local currency through an off-ramp service. This is usually where most of the real cost hides — the spread here is noticeably higher than at the exchange.

Your final amount is the starting sum minus the losses at both stages combined. Run your own combination through the withdrawal route tool — it already accounts for both stages and shows what you'd get for each route.

Cash-out services: USDT to rubles

The figures below come straight from each provider's own published fee page (not a blog or an estimate), but neither publishes a public API for their tariffs — verify before a large withdrawal, since fees can change. Full comparison: "Compare Cash-Out Services".

Whitebird — a licensed platform (Belarus) that converts USDT/BTC/ETH to rubles or Belarusian rubles onto a Mir card. Withdrawing to a Russian bank card runs 2.0% (whitebird.io/commission).

Cifra Markets — a brokerage platform for CIS-based traders. 1.5% to convert USDT/RUB plus a 500 RUB withdrawal fee for non-Belarus residents (free if withdrawing into a Cifra Bank account), cifra.by/rates.

E-wallets as an intermediate step

Payeer and AdvCash / Volet no longer support direct transfers to Russian bank cards on their own, but they're still useful as an intermediate step between an exchange and an off-ramp — for example, if a particular off-ramp doesn't accept USDT directly from your exchange. Whether either has an affiliate program is still unverified.

Bank transfer — for contrast

Bank transfer remains technically available, and it is shown among the routes above purely for contrast: a bank typically bakes a rate markup well below mid-market into the transfer, plus a flat fee. Compared to crypto routes, it's usually the most expensive option in absolute terms.
