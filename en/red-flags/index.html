Prop Firm Red Flags

The prop firm industry isn't licensed by any government — legally, a "prop firm license" doesn't exist anywhere (more in the glossary). Between February 2024 and the end of 2025, an estimated 80 to 100 prop firms shut down — called the largest collapse in the industry's history. Here's what to look for before paying for an evaluation at an unfamiliar firm.

Payout problems

No public proof of payouts
Only claims on the firm's own site, with no screenshots, videos, or confirmation from real third-party traders.
Withdrawal rules change after the fact
New restrictions or conditions appear only after you've passed the evaluation and got a funded account.
Promised payout timelines aren't kept
The site says "payout within 24 hours," but requests actually sit for weeks with no real explanation.

Opaque rules

"Violations at the firm's discretion"
Wording with no concrete numbers or criteria — the firm decides after the fact what counts as a violation.
Rules change without warning
And this is written into the user agreement as the norm, not an exception.
Hidden conditions only surface at withdrawal
A restriction nobody mentioned beforehand suddenly appears right when you request a payout.

Signs of a non-serious company

No registered legal entity
Just a website and social media — no address, no company name, no registration number listed anywhere.
Under 6 months old with no public track record
Not a dealbreaker by itself — but a reason for extra caution until there's a longer payout history.
Unprofessional support communication
Obvious mistakes in writing, an odd tone, evasive answers that don't actually address the question.

Reputation manipulation

A sudden burst of near-identical 5-star reviews
A lot of similarly worded positive reviews in a short window is a common sign of seeded reviews.
Promises of "guaranteed profit" or "no risk"
No honest prop firm — or any honest market participant — can guarantee that. It's marketing, not a fact.

How we apply this

On the "Compare Prop Firms" page, we use concrete criteria instead of marketing claims: a confirmed multi-year payout history — not just the firm's own statements, a Trustpilot rating of 4.3+ with a large review count, no retroactive rule changes for already-funded traders, and ideally backing by a regulated broker.

Some firms in the comparison don't fully meet every criterion — one has a Trustpilot score below our benchmark, another has few reviews because it's young. We don't hide that: that firm's page carries a "Why it's listed" box stating plainly what raises questions, with a recommendation to weigh the risk yourself.

How to check a firm yourself

  1. Look past the Trustpilot score itself at the volume and pattern of reviews — a sudden burst of similar 5-star reviews in a short window is worth a second look.
  2. Search "[firm name] scam" and "withdrawal problems" — forums and social media from real traders are often more honest than the official site.
  3. Check how old the firm is and whether it has a public payout history spanning years, not just the last couple of months.
  4. Read the violation rules — do they use concrete numbers and criteria, or vague "at the firm's discretion" language?
  5. Check whether the firm has a registered legal entity, and where.
  6. Be wary of any promise of guaranteed profit or "no risk."
